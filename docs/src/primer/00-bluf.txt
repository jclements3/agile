== Bottom line up front {#bluf} @before-contents

**{{value: docs/tidewater/work-split.json s1_6.engineering}}% of the work the team finished in the first twelve weeks
is actual systems engineering, and the share rises sprint by sprint.** The rest is a one-time investment in tools and
learning: {{value: docs/tidewater/work-split.json sprints.1.overhead}}% of sprint 1, down to
{{value: docs/tidewater/work-split.json sprints.6.overhead}}% of sprint 6. The figure for the whole eighteen months
follows in the next edition, when the later sprints have run.

If you read nothing else in this book, read this page. It is written for the people who have to approve the
change, and for the engineers who have to live with it.

=== Where the team's work goes

Every story on the team's board carries one of three labels. *Engineering* is work on the system itself:
requirements, structure, behavior, interfaces, verification. *Platform* is the DevSecOps machinery that checks and
publishes that work: the GitLab project, the pipeline, the runner. *Learning* is time the team spends learning
the new tools, budgeted on purpose so it does not happen on nights and weekends.

=== Where all the time goes, meetings included

Points cover the work on the board. The agile meetings come on top: a fifteen-minute stand-up each day, a kickoff,
a refinement session, and a review with a retrospective at the end of each sprint. Together they take about
{{value: docs/tidewater/work-split.json ceremony_hours.total}} hours per person per sprint, or
{{value: docs/tidewater/work-split.json ceremony_hours.share_of_sprint}}% of an eighty-hour sprint.

=== What this means

- **The overhead is real, visible and temporary.** Platform and learning work dominate the first two sprints,
  because the team is building the shop and learning to use it. By sprint 6 most of the board is engineering
  again. A plan that hides this cost is a plan that fails in week three.
- **The meetings cost less than a tenth of the team's time,** and they replace status meetings, review meetings and
  the slide-building that used to come before every milestone.
- **The engineering that remains is faster and checked.** Every change is checked automatically and reviewed
  before it is accepted. Problems the team used to find at the next milestone review are found the same day. The
  measured results -- requirements traced, model items ported, review turnaround, problems caught early -- are in
  [[scorecard]], and the briefing the team gave management at the end of the twelve weeks is in [[briefing]].
- **What management must provide** is the learning time in this table, a runner machine, the tool approvals and
  a GitLab tier decision, asked for in writing in sprint 2 ([[memos]]).

::: note Where these numbers come from
The tables are computed from the team's plan (`sim/tidewater-plan.pl`) by `perl sim/tidewater-split.pl`, and the

simulation's results replace the plan in that file, these tables follow. The method is described in [[se-work]];
you can apply it to your own project's backlog in [[playbook]].
