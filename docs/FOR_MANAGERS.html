How the teams plan and report work

The basic idea, on one page, for managers.

The rhythm

Work is planned in two-week blocks called sprints. Every sprint has the same shape:

Day 1Each team commits to a short list of tasks it can finish in two weeks. The list is sized against the team's capacity, so nobody commits to more than they can do.
DailyOne 15-minute all-hands check-in at a fixed time. Everyone answers three questions in the meeting chat: what did you finish yesterday, what are you doing today, is anything in your way. Nobody gives a speech.
Day 8Mid-sprint check: are we on track, what is stuck.
Day 14Review: teams show what is finished. Anything not finished is carried into the next sprint, visibly. Then planning again.

What counts

A task is either done or not done. There is no partial credit and no "90% complete" — if a task is too big to finish in a sprint it is split into smaller ones. This keeps the numbers honest.

Work is organized in three layers:

Taskfinishes inside one sprint (two weeks)
Epica group of tasks; takes several sprints (under six months)
Tomea major objective made of epics; up to two years

What you will receive

DailyA three-line status: green amber red, one sentence, and at most five items that need attention or a decision.
WeeklyThe same, plus the trend: how much each team is finishing per sprint and how reliably.
Each sprintA one-page report: planned vs finished, what carried over, and a roadmap showing when each epic is expected to land based on the teams' actual pace.
On requestA history of every sprint to date, as a PDF.
Every number in these reports is calculated from the same record the teams update daily. Nothing is typed into a slide by hand, so the figures cannot disagree with each other.

What the numbers mean

Done %finished work as a share of what was committed.
Velocityhow much a team finishes per sprint, averaged over the last three. Used to forecast dates.
Predictabilityhow close teams come to finishing what they commit. Above 80% is healthy.
Blockeda task waiting on something outside the team. Anything blocked more than three days is escalated to me; more than five and it will be in your weekly report by name.

Where you come in

You will rarely need to ask for status; it arrives. What I will bring to you are decisions: a blocker that needs your authority, a trade-off between scope and date, or a case where more work is coming in than the teams can finish — with the number of sprints it represents, so the choice of what to cut is yours.
