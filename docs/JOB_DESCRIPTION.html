Solutions Architect

Multi-team agile program — job description

Summary

The Solutions Architect owns the technical shape of a whole solution built by several agile teams. They make the cross-cutting decisions no single team can make alone — structure, interfaces, non-functional requirements — and keep those decisions just far enough ahead of delivery that no team is blocked.

RoleScope
Enterprise ArchitectPortfolio-wide: technology strategy, standards, platforms
Solutions ArchitectOne solution, often a system of systems built by several teams or trains
System ArchitectOne system or one team-of-teams

In SAFe terms it is the Solution Architect at the Large Solution level, working alongside the Solution Train Engineer (process) and Solution Management (content). This is an agile architecture role: "just enough, just in time". The architect sets guardrails and interface contracts and lets design detail emerge in the teams; they coach and negotiate rather than hand down finished designs.

Responsibilities

Intentional architecture

  • Define the high-level structure, subsystem boundaries, interfaces and non-functional requirements (performance, security, safety, certification) so that many teams can build independently without integration collapse.
  • Maintain the function model of the solution (IDEF0 or equivalent) and derive the product backlog from it: models to tomes and teams, activities to epics, leaf activities to tasks.

Interfaces and integration

  • Own every cross-team contract. Each cross-model interface is a tracked work item assigned to the consuming team under the producing epic; epics that span teams are the integration points the architect watches daily.
  • Chair the cross-team dependency check and clear or escalate any interface blocker older than three days.

Architectural runway and enablers

  • Keep enabling infrastructure a few increments ahead of feature work without big design up front.
  • Write and prioritize enablers (spikes, infrastructure, refactors) in the master backlog and refine them into team backlogs.

Backlog ownership

  • Own the master backlog: intake, sizing, priority within each tome, refinement into team backlogs, pruning of stale work.
  • Enforce the definitions of Ready and Done. A task is pass/fail against Done; there is no partial credit — split the task instead.

Cadence and operating rhythm

  • Run the daily all-teams townhall (fixed 15-minute window, chat is the record): everyone answers yesterday / today / blockers in the meeting chat; the architect reads the derived flags, not the chat.
  • Run sprint planning (Day 1), the mid-sprint health check (Day 8), master-backlog refinement (Day 9) and the sprint review/retro (Day 14) on a two-week cycle.

Measurement, forecasting and reporting

  • Maintain the single plain-text journal from which every metric is derived, never retyped: committed/done, velocity, predictability, load, carryover rate, blocked count and age, backlog depth and age, intake vs done, epic and tome progress.
  • Produce the daily one-screen leadership brief (BLUF: one line of status, one sentence, at most five action items), the daily status mail to leads, the weekly and sprint reports, and the epic roadmap (actuals from the journal, forecast from team velocity).
  • Keep the sprint history as the audit trail; export it for program reviews.

Trade-offs

  • Balance cost, schedule, risk and technical debt with product management and systems engineering; own estimates that combine task assignments with time-varying labor rates.

Information handling

  • Apply the organisation's information markings on every report, mail and PDF (banner, marking block, subject prefix); send marked material only where the organisation allows, encrypted in transit.

Qualifications

  • Experience as an architect or lead engineer across several agile teams on a system-of-systems; SAFe Large Solution experience or equivalent.
  • Function/process modeling (IDEF0) or MBSE, and familiarity with work-breakdown structures and stage-gated programme lifecycles.
  • Comfortable working from a plain Windows workstation with Git for Windows, Vim and core Perl only (nothing to install); Teams and classic Outlook.
  • Clear, terse written communication for leadership.

Measures of success

  • Interface blockers cleared within three days; none older than five.
  • Predictability (done / committed over the last three sprints) at or above 80% per team; load between 70% and 110% at planning.
  • Carryover rate under 20%; backlog depth of two to four sprints per team; no backlog item older than a quarter without a decision.
  • Leadership never has to ask for status: the brief goes out daily, the roadmap is current, the history is exportable.

"Solutions Architect" is also a pre-sales title at cloud vendors, where it means designing customer deployments with the vendor's products. That is a different job from this one.
