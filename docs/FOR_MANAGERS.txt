HOW THE TEAMS PLAN AND REPORT WORK -- THE BASIC IDEA
One page for managers

THE RHYTHM

Work is planned in two-week blocks called sprints. Every sprint has the
same shape:

  Day 1     Each team commits to a short list of tasks it can finish in
            two weeks. The list is sized against the team's capacity, so
            nobody commits to more than they can do.
  Daily     One 15-minute all-hands check-in at a fixed time. Everyone
            answers three questions in the meeting chat: what did you
            finish yesterday, what are you doing today, is anything in
            your way. Nobody gives a speech.
  Day 8     Mid-sprint check: are we on track, what is stuck.
  Day 14    Review: teams show what is finished. Anything not finished is
            carried into the next sprint, visibly. Then planning again.

WHAT COUNTS

A task is either done or not done. There is no partial credit and no
"90% complete" -- if a task is too big to finish in a sprint it is split
into smaller ones. This keeps the numbers honest.

Work is organized in three layers:
  Task    finishes inside one sprint (two weeks)
  Epic    a group of tasks; takes several sprints (under six months)
  Tome    a major objective made of epics; up to two years

WHAT YOU WILL RECEIVE

  Daily      A three-line status: green / amber / red, one sentence, and
             at most five items that need attention or a decision.
  Weekly     The same, plus the trend: how much each team is finishing
             per sprint and how reliably.
  Each sprint  A one-page report per sprint: planned vs finished, what
             carried over, and a roadmap showing when each epic is
             expected to land based on the teams' actual pace.
  On request A history of every sprint to date, as a PDF.

Every number in these reports is calculated from the same record the
teams update daily. Nothing is typed into a slide by hand, so the
figures cannot disagree with each other.

WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN

  Done %          finished work as a share of what was committed.
  Velocity        how much a team finishes per sprint, averaged over
                  the last three. Used to forecast dates.
  Predictability  how close teams come to finishing what they commit.
                  Above 80% is healthy.
  Blocked         a task waiting on something outside the team. Anything
                  blocked more than three days is escalated to me;
                  more than five and it will be in your weekly report
                  by name.

WHERE YOU COME IN

You will rarely need to ask for status; it arrives. What I will bring
to you are decisions: a blocker that needs your authority, a trade-off
between scope and date, or a case where more work is coming in than the
teams can finish -- with the number of sprints it represents, so the
choice of what to cut is yours.
