== The rhythm: stand-ups, poker, refinement, kickoff, demo, retro {#ceremonies}

::: survival
What you have: a journal of stories ([[journal]]), tasks in GitLab ([[tools]]), and two-week sprints.

What you need: the meetings that keep the plan honest, run in a way three systems engineers can sustain: a daily
stand-up of terse written statuses, planning poker for estimates, a mid-sprint refinement, a kickoff on day one and a
demo and retrospective on the last day. Each meeting produces a record the kit reads, so nobody writes minutes. This
chapter shows each one with real chat pastes and the kit's real output.
:::

=== Why meetings at all {#ceremonies-why}

The team's first reaction to the word "ceremonies" was predictable.

> We already have too many meetings. Now we get a daily one and four more every two weeks?
> -- Tom, week zero

The answer the team arrived at, after a sprint of trying it, was that these meetings *replace* others rather than add
to them. The weekly status meeting, the action item review, the monthly schedule review and most of the e-mail chains
went away, because their questions were answered in fifteen minutes a day and two hours a fortnight, from records the
meetings themselves produced.

|| When | Meeting | Length | Produces | Replaces ||
| Daily, 09:00 | stand-up | 15 minutes | Y/T/B statuses; blocks; done stories | the weekly status meeting, the action item review |
| Day 1 (Monday, week 1) | sprint kickoff and planning | 90 minutes | the sprint goal; capacity; committed stories; task issues | the monthly schedule review |
| Wednesday, week 2 | refinement | 1 hour | the next sprint's candidate stories, estimated and ready | ad hoc tasking e-mails |
| Day 10 (Friday, week 2) | demo (sprint review) | 60 minutes | what was shown, feedback, accepted stories | review-by-screenshot briefings |
| Day 10, after the demo | retrospective | 60 minutes | one or two changes; the pulse survey | the annual lessons-learned exercise |
| Every Friday | weekly status | 0 minutes of meeting | the quad, sent to management | the weekly status slides |
Table: The two-week rhythm

=== The daily stand-up: terse, written, linted {#ceremonies-standup}

The team's stand-up is a Teams meeting at 09:00 whose main event is the chat. Each person posts one line with three
parts, **Y** (what I did yesterday), **T** (what I will do today) and **B** (what blocks me), each naming the story it
is about:

```text
Y TW-4 read the converter report T TW-4 TODO inventory by package B none
```

The kit reads two shapes. In sprints 1 and 2 the team posted the three parts on three lines, as most people do at
first; this is Tidewater's first stand-up, from the meeting chat:

{{include: docs/tidewater/sprints/01/standups/2026-10-05-chat.txt}}

From sprint 3 they posted one line per person, drafted by `talk.pl` ([[talking]]) and edited before posting, with the
outcome and a number first. The examples in this section use the one-line shape because it is shorter to type; the
lint treats both the same way.

Then they talk only about the blockers and anything that needs two people. The statuses are the record; the talk is
for help.

Why written, and why so terse? Because a written status can be checked by a program, compiled into the journal and
compared with yesterday's, and a spoken one cannot. And because three engineers who had spent years writing status
paragraphs for slides found that one line, with an id, said more.

The rules, from the team's wiki:

- **Every part is present.** `B none` when nothing blocks you; an empty B is not "none", it is a missing answer.
- **Every Y and T names a story id** (`TW-4`). Work with no id is invisible in the plan; if it matters, it should be a
  story.
- **A blocker goes in B, not in T.** "Waiting on the admins" is not something you will *do* today.
- **Capital Y, T, B as delimiters**; lower-case letters inside the text are just letters.

==== The lint

To type along, you need a journal with sprint 1 planned. If you made one in [[journal]], use it; otherwise these
commands make the same one in a few seconds:

```sh run sandbox=scratch expect="Model   19      17"
mkdir -p ~/tidewater-journal && cd ~/tidewater-journal
if [ ! -f scrum.txt ]; then                 # only if you do not have the journal from the last chapter
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl init . > /dev/null
perl -pi -e 's/^default_team\s*=\s*/default_team  = Model   /' scrum.conf
for who in Jim Bob Tom; do perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl roster add "$who" $(echo $who | tr A-Z a-z)@example.invalid Model Engineer Gov; done
mkdir -p standups && cat > standups/2026-10-05.txt <<'PLAN'
2026-10-05
sprint 1
== Model
cap 19
new TW-1 4 Create the GitLab group and model project p:1 e:"E1 GitLab project and gitflow" t:"T1 Stand up MBSE DevSecOps" o:Tom
new TW-2 4 First pipeline gates every MR p:1 e:"E2 Pipeline and quality gates" t:"T1 Stand up MBSE DevSecOps" o:Tom
new TW-3 3 Receive drop 1 and record it p:1 e:"E5 Drop 1 intake and first import" t:"T2 Port Tidewater v1 to SysML v2" o:Jim
new TW-4 4 Convert drop 1 and assess it p:1 e:"E5 Drop 1 intake and first import" t:"T2 Port Tidewater v1 to SysML v2" o:Bob
new TW-5 2 Working agreements p:2 e:"E3 Team practices and onboarding" t:"T1 Stand up MBSE DevSecOps" o:Jim
commit TW-1 Tom
commit TW-2 Tom
commit TW-3 Jim
commit TW-4 Bob
commit TW-5 Jim
PLAN
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl compile
fi
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl --today 2026-10-06 sprint | head -4
```

While people are still on the call, Jim pastes the chat into a file and runs the kit's **lint** on it: a check of
every status against the rules. Here is the chat of Tuesday 6 October, sprint 1, day 2, the team's first real
stand-up. Jim pasted it into `standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt` (in Vim, `\sp` does the paste; from the shell, a
here-document does the same):

```sh run cwd=~/tidewater-journal expect="ERROR Tom: B is empty"
cat > standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt <<'CHAT'
[9:01 AM] Jim
Y TW-3 logged the CDRL delivery, checksums match T TW-3 move drop 1 to approved storage, TW-5 draft DoR B none
[9:02 AM] Tom
Y requested the group and a runner T TW-1 waiting on the admins B
[9:03 AM] Bob
yesterday: read the converter report. today: TODO inventory. blockers: none
CHAT
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl --today 2026-10-06 lint standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt || echo "exit status $?"
```

```text
standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt:2: ok    Jim: Y/T/B parsed
standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt:4: ERROR Tom: B is empty (write 'none')
standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt:6: warn  Bob: no task id in Y (e.g. TW-1); no task id in T (e.g. TW-1)
# 3 answered, 1 error, 1 warning
```

The lint reads Bob's longhand correctly but warns that there are no story ids. It refuses Tom's status: the B is
empty, and the blocker is sitting in the T. The `--reply` option writes the lines to paste back into the chat, so
people fix their own statuses during the call:

```sh run expect="Hey Tom! I couldn't read your status"
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl --today 2026-10-06 lint standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt --reply || echo "exit status $?"
```

```text
Hey Tom! I couldn't read your status (B is empty (write 'none')). Repost as: Y did TW-1 T doing TW-2 B none
Hey Bob! Got it. Add the task id next time so it counts (e.g. Y did TW-4 T doing TW-4 B none).
```

Tom and Bob reposted, and Jim pasted the new lines under the old ones:

```sh run expect="3 answered, 0 errors, 0 warnings"
cat >> standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt <<'CHAT'
[9:06 AM] Tom
Y TW-1 requested the group and a runner T TW-2 draft the first .gitlab-ci.yml B TW-1 waiting on the enterprise GitLab admins
[9:07 AM] Bob
Y TW-4 read the converter report T TW-4 TODO inventory by package B none
CHAT
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl --today 2026-10-06 lint standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt
```

and the second lint was clean (the parser keeps each person's last status):

```text
standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt:2: ok    Jim: Y/T/B parsed
standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt:8: ok    Tom: Y/T/B parsed
standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt:10: ok    Bob: Y/T/B parsed
# 3 answered, 0 errors, 0 warnings
```

`--table` shows what the parser understood, one row per person, which is how you check that it read what you meant:

```sh run expect="ok\tTom\tTW-1 requested the group and a runner"
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl --today 2026-10-06 lint standups/2026-10-06-chat.txt --table
```

```text
state	who	Y	T	B	note
ok	Jim	TW-3 logged the CDRL delivery, checksums match	TW-3 move drop 1 to approved storage, TW-5 draft DoR	none
ok	Tom	TW-1 requested the group and a runner	TW-2 draft the first .gitlab-ci.yml	TW-1 waiting on the enterprise GitLab admins
ok	Bob	TW-4 read the converter report	TW-4 TODO inventory by package	none
```

In the first two weeks the lint flagged something almost every day. By sprint 3 the statuses were clean, and the
kit's read-backs (a confirmation line for each clean status) stopped by themselves after a run of clean days, as
`scrum.conf`'s `readback_clean_days` sets.

In Vim, the whole loop is one layout: `:STown` opens the chat buffer, the lint table and the replies side by side;
`\sp` pastes and lints, `\sy` copies the replies to the clipboard. [[vim]] has the details.

==== After the call: answers and flags

After the call, `daily.pl answers` reads the day's chat, writes the statuses into the record, raises **flags**, and
appends suggested journal lines to today's stand-up file for Jim to confirm:

```sh run expect="AMBER Tom +blocked: TW-1 waiting on the enterprise GitLab admins"
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl --today 2026-10-06 answers
```

```text
Model stand-up answers: 3 people, 1 blocked
  Jim            Y: TW-3 logged the CDRL delivery, checksums match
                 T: TW-3 move drop 1 to approved storage, TW-5 draft DoR
                 B: none
  Tom            Y: TW-1 requested the group and a runner
                 T: TW-2 draft the first .gitlab-ci.yml
                 B: TW-1 waiting on the enterprise GitLab admins
  Bob            Y: TW-4 read the converter report
                 T: TW-4 TODO inventory by package
                 B: none
flags:
  AMBER Tom            blocked: TW-1 waiting on the enterprise GitLab admins
wrote standups/2026-10-06-Model-answers.txt
  attendance: 3 rows (3 answered) -> attendance.csv
  appended suggested lines to standups/2026-10-06.txt
```

The suggested line in the stand-up file was `block TW-1 waiting on the enterprise GitLab admins`. Jim compiled it,
and the block was on the record. The flags grow with the days: a blocker reported two days running is amber, three
days red; the same "today" three days running is amber ("is this stuck?"); a story id that is not in the sprint, or
not in the journal at all, is flagged; someone who says "done" in Y gets a suggested `done` line to confirm. None of
these is applied automatically. The kit suggests, the lead confirms.

::: sidebar What the terse format did for the team
By the end of sprint 2 Bob, who had complained loudest about "status theater", said the one-line status was the first
status format that had ever told Bob anything about the other two's work. It also made the blockers impossible to
bury: the enterprise administrators' slow runner request was red on the weekly report by its third day, and
management called the administrators before the team had to.
:::

=== Planning poker {#ceremonies-poker}

**Planning poker** is a way to estimate that avoids the loudest voice setting the number. Everyone votes at the same
time, in points, without seeing the others' votes; if the votes differ, the highest and lowest explain their reasons,
and the team votes again. The discussion is the point: a wide spread means people understand the story differently.

The team plays it in the meeting chat with the kit's `#est` rounds. Jim posts `#est` and the story; each person posts
a number; the kit tallies the round from the pasted chat. Here is the refinement session of Wednesday 14 October,
sprint 1, week 2, with three candidate stories for sprint 2:

```sh run expect="(?m)^3$"
cat > standups/2026-10-14-chat.txt <<'CHAT'
[10:02 AM] Jim
Refinement. Three candidates for sprint 2. Vote in points: 1 point = one ideal day.
#est TW-6 import drop 1 on vendor/prime and merge to develop
[10:03 AM] Bob
3
[10:03 AM] Tom
3
[10:03 AM] Jim
2
[10:05 AM] Jim
#est TW-7 docs, outline and TODO jobs
[10:06 AM] Tom
3
[10:06 AM] Bob
8
[10:06 AM] Jim
3
[10:09 AM] Bob
the docs job means generating docs for the first time, model.pl docs warned on our model. that is not 3 days
[10:12 AM] Jim
#est TW-7 docs, outline and TODO jobs (revote)
[10:13 AM] Tom
5
[10:13 AM] Bob
5
[10:13 AM] Jim
5
[10:15 AM] Jim
#vote Split TW-7 so the docs job is its own story? y/n
[10:15 AM] Bob
y
[10:15 AM] Tom
n
[10:16 AM] Jim
n
CHAT
grep -c "#est" standups/2026-10-14-chat.txt      # three rounds
```

```sh run expect="result: n \(majority\)"
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl --today 2026-10-14 chat standups/2026-10-14-chat.txt
```

```text
ESTIMATE TW-6 import drop 1 on vendor/prime and merge to develop  (3 answers at 10:02)
  Bob            3
  Jim            2
  Tom            3
  no consensus: median 3, mode 3, range 2-3

ESTIMATE TW-7 docs, outline and TODO jobs  (3 answers at 10:05)
  Bob            8
  Jim            3
  Tom            3
  no consensus: median 3, mode 3, range 3-8 — re-discuss with Bob

ESTIMATE TW-7 docs, outline and TODO jobs (revote)  (3 answers at 10:12)
  Bob            5
  Jim            5
  Tom            5
  consensus: 5

VOTE Split TW-7 so the docs job is its own story?  (3 votes at 10:15)
  n           2  Jim, Tom
  y           1  Bob
  result: n (majority)
appended to standups/2026-10-14.txt (uncomment the est lines you accept; note lines apply as-is)
```

Three things in that output are worth slowing down for.

**The real disagreement was on TW-7.** Bob voted 8 against two 3s, and explained: generating the model documents for
the first time had produced warnings, so the docs job was not a routine job. After Bob's explanation the team voted 5,
unanimously. The estimate moved because of information, not pressure. That is what poker is for.

**Bob's explanation did not count as a vote.** It contained a number ("that is not 3 days"), but the kit reads
only answers as votes: a number with an optional unit, optionally after "actually" or "make it" and the like. Anything
else in a round is discussion, so Bob's 8 stood, and the tally flagged it for re-discussion ("re-discuss with Bob").
People can still change their vote by posting a new answer ("actually 5"); the latest answer wins.

**The kit suggests; Jim decides.** The tally appends lines to the day's stand-up file, commented out, for Jim to
accept by removing the semicolon:

```text
; ---- chat 2026-10-14
== Model
; est TW-6 3   ; median, no consensus: Bob 3, Jim 2, Tom 3
; est TW-7 3   ; median, no consensus: Bob 8, Jim 3, Tom 3
; est TW-7 5   ; consensus: Bob 5, Jim 5, Tom 5   ; task not in journal yet
note vote Split TW-7 so the docs job is its own story? -> n (n 2, y 1)
```

Jim accepted the revote (`est TW-7 5`) and left the others commented. TW-6 stayed at the 3 it was proposed at.

::: note Which team section the lines go under
Est lines for tasks the journal already knows go under that task's team. The rest -- stories not filed yet, like
TW-7 here, and the vote notes -- go under `default_team` from `scrum.conf` (the team set it to `Model` in week
zero), or under the team in the chat file's name (`standups/DATE-Model-chat.txt`). Look at the end of the file the
tally wrote:

```sh run expect="(?m)^== Model\s*$"
tail -6 standups/2026-10-14.txt
```

With neither set, the tally says so (`chat: no team for est lines of tasks not in the journal yet`) and leaves those
lines under a bare `==`, where an uncommented `est` line would fail the compile. Set `default_team` once and the
question never comes up.
:::

==== How big is a point?

The team's scale was 1 point = one ideal person-day: a day with no meetings and no interruptions. A task may be at
most 5 points (under a week); a story is a few tasks and must fit in one sprint; Jim's capacity was about 5 points a
sprint (the rest of Jim's time went to management), Bob's and Tom's about 8. Using ideal days rather than abstract
"story points" was a concession to the team's background: systems engineers estimate in days, and pretending
otherwise would have cost more trust than it bought.

=== Refinement and the Definition of Ready {#ceremonies-refinement}

**Refinement** (also called backlog grooming) prepares the next sprint's stories so planning is quick. It happens on
the Wednesday of the second week, so there is time to fix what it finds before Monday. Jim brings candidate stories;
the team splits them, estimates them, and checks each against the **Definition of Ready**, kept in the wiki and in the
Story template ([[gitlab-setup]]):

::: checklist Definition of Ready (a story may enter a sprint when)
[ ] it fits in one sprint and names its epic and tome
[ ] its acceptance criteria are written so someone who did not do the work can check them
[ ] its tasks are cut, each at most 5 points (under a week)
[ ] its dependencies on the prime or on another story are linked
[ ] it has been estimated in poker, and the spread was explained
:::

A ready story gets the `workflow::ready` label in GitLab. The refinement's journal file brings the new stories in with
`new` lines (into the backlog, not yet committed), plus the accepted `est` lines:

```sh run expect="TW-7   5     1"
cat > standups/2026-10-14.txt <<'REF'
2026-10-14
sprint 1
== Model
note Refinement: sprint 2 candidates, Definition of Ready applied
new TW-6 3 Import drop 1 on vendor/prime and merge it to develop p:1 e:"E5 Drop 1 intake and first import" t:"T2 Port Tidewater v1 to SysML v2" o:Bob
new TW-7 4 Docs, outline and TODO jobs p:1 e:"E2 Pipeline and quality gates" t:"T1 Stand up MBSE DevSecOps" o:Tom
new TW-8 3 Level conventions p:2 e:"E6 Structure: close the structure TODOs" t:"T2 Port Tidewater v1 to SysML v2" o:Bob
est TW-7 5
note vote Split TW-7 so the docs job is its own story? -> n (n 2, y 1)
REF
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl compile
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl --today 2026-10-14 backlog Model
```

(Jim rewrote the day's file with the refinement lines and the accepted estimate, replacing the tally's commented
suggestions.) After compiling, the backlog showed the three stories with TW-7 at its new 5 points:

```text

ID    SP  Prio  Tome                              Epic                                     Owner  Age  Title                                                  Blocked
----  --  ----  --------------------------------  ---------------------------------------  -----  ---  -----------------------------------------------------  -------
TW-6   3     1  T2 Port Tidewater v1 to SysML v2  E5 Drop 1 intake and first import        Bob      0  Import drop 1 on vendor/prime and merge it to develop
TW-7   5     1  T1 Stand up MBSE DevSecOps        E2 Pipeline and quality gates            Tom      0  Docs, outline and TODO jobs
```

=== Kickoff and planning {#ceremonies-kickoff}

The sprint starts on Monday morning with ninety minutes that do three things:

1. **The goal.** One sentence that says what the sprint is *for*, in terms management would recognize. Sprint 1's:
   "A GitLab project the team can work in; drop 1 received and assessed." A goal lets the team make trade-offs during
   the sprint without asking: anything that serves the goal wins.
2. **Capacity.** Each person's available days, minus holidays, leave and known meetings, summed into the `cap` line.
   Sprint 1 lost a day to the Columbus Day holiday; sprint 4 lost Thanksgiving and a day of Jim's leave.
3. **Commitment.** The team pulls ready stories, highest priority first, until the total is about 85 to 95% of
   capacity. Each committed story gets the sprint's milestone in GitLab and its tasks are created (or confirmed) as
   task issues; the journal gets the `cap` and `commit` lines ([[journal]] shows sprint 1's file).

The kickoff ends with the board reset: last sprint's closed cards gone, this sprint's stories in `workflow::ready`.

=== The demo {#ceremonies-demo}

The **sprint review**, or demo, is where the team shows working results to the people who care about them:
management, the deputy program manager who chairs the configuration control board, sometimes the prime's modeler.
The rule that made the team's demos work was borrowed from the document era's worst habit and turned inside out:

> Show the thing, not a slide about the thing.
> -- Jim's note on the demo agenda, sprint 1

So the demo shows real artifacts, in the tools: the merge request with its pipeline widgets, the board, the TODO count
falling, the trace report, from sprint 4 the model website. Sprint 1's demo showed the project and its protections,
a merge request blocked by a lint failure, and the drop 1 TODO inventory of 52 lines. It took twenty minutes.

At the end of the demo, Jim marks the stories the audience accepted as done in the journal, and runs the sprint
report:

```sh run expect="Sprint 1 report -- as of 2026-10-16: 17/17 SP done \(100%\)"
printf '2026-10-16\nsprint 1\n== Model\ndone TW-1 TW-2 TW-3 TW-4 TW-5\nnote Sprint 1 review: all five accepted\n' > standups/2026-10-16.txt
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl compile
perl $KIT/bin/daily.pl --today 2026-10-16 review 1
```

```text
Sprint 1 report -- as of 2026-10-16: 17/17 SP done (100%), 0 carried over, 0 removed.

Team           Commit  Done  Done%  Carry Carry%   Load Velocity  Predict%  Punted Interrupt    Cap
Model              17    17   100%      0     0%    89%     17.0      100%       0         0     19

Epics (remaining / team velocity = sprints to done):
  T1 Stand up MBSE DevSecOps > E1 GitLab project and gitflow  100%     0 SP left  ETA sprint 1
  T1 Stand up MBSE DevSecOps > E2 Pipeline and quality gates   44%     5 SP left  ETA sprint 2
  T1 Stand up MBSE DevSecOps > E3 Team practices and onboarding  100%     0 SP left  ETA sprint 1
  T2 Port Tidewater v1 to SysML v2 > E5 Drop 1 intake and first import   70%     3 SP left  ETA sprint 2
  T2 Port Tidewater v1 to SysML v2 > E6 Structure: close the structure TODOs    0%     3 SP left  ETA sprint 2
wrote reports/sprint-1-report.txt
wrote reports/sprint-1-report.html
```

(from the practice journal; the real sprint 1 is in [[sprint-1]]). Unfinished stories are either carried into the next
sprint (`carry`) or returned to the backlog, never quietly left open.

=== The retrospective {#ceremonies-retro}

The **retrospective** is the team talking to itself about how it works, not what it built. The format the team used:

1. **The pulse survey**, five questions answered anonymously on a 1 to 5 scale ([[directive]] defines them):
   I understand why we are working this way; I can do my work in the new tools without help; the checks catch problems
   I would have missed; I can see our progress; I would choose to keep working this way. Five minutes, on paper or a
   form. The averages go on the scorecard ([[scorecard]]).
2. **Start, stop, continue.** Each person writes what the team should start doing, stop doing and keep doing.
3. **One or two changes.** Not ten. Each change becomes a story or a working-agreement edit, with an owner.
4. **Last sprint's changes.** Did they help? Keep or drop.

The retrospective is the one meeting where nothing is recorded in the journal except the survey average and the
changes. What people say stays in the room. That rule mattered: the team was discouraged at the start ([[directive]]),
and the retrospective had to be a place to say so.

The changes that came out of the first six retrospectives (the report widgets, the ratchet, the "keep numbers out of
chat comments" rule, the second runner) are told in Part III, and the pulse survey's arc in [[minds]].

=== Exercises {#ceremonies-exercises}

::: exercise {#ex-cer-lint} Lint your own status
Write yesterday's work as a Y/T/B line in a chat file, in the Teams copy format above, with one deliberate mistake.
Run `daily.pl lint` on it, then `--reply`. Fix it until the lint is clean.
:::

::: exercise {#ex-cer-poker} Play a round
With two colleagues, estimate three of your real tasks in an `#est` chat and tally it with `daily.pl chat`. Where did
the spread come from? Did discussion change the number?
:::

::: exercise {#ex-cer-ready} Apply the Definition of Ready
Take a work item from your current plan and check it against the Definition of Ready. What would you have to do to make
it ready? Which item failed first?
:::

::: exercise {#ex-cer-demo} Plan a demo without slides
Write the agenda for a twenty-minute demo of your team's last two weeks that shows only real artifacts. What would you
show, in what order, and what question do you want the audience to answer?
:::

=== Applying this to your project {#ceremonies-apply}

- **Copy**: terse written statuses with an id in every part, linted during the call; poker in the chat with a revote
  after the outliers explain; refinement mid-sprint against a written Definition of Ready; a one-sentence sprint goal;
  demos that show the thing; a short retrospective with a pulse survey and one or two changes; a weekly one-page status
  generated, not written.
- **Adapt**: the times (a team across time zones may need asynchronous stand-ups in the chat alone); the scale (ideal
  days or abstract points, but pick one and keep it); who attends the demo.
- **Decide locally**: what goes upward and what stays in the team; who confirms the kit's suggested lines; which
  meetings these replace, agreed with management in writing so the calendar actually gets lighter.
