= Part III: Twelve weeks

Part II set up the shop: the operating model, the laptops, git, Vim, GitLab, the pipeline, the journal and the
meetings. Part III is the work itself. Each of the next six chapters is one two-week sprint of Jim, Bob and Tom's
first twelve weeks, from Monday 5 October to Friday 18 December 2026, told from the record the sprint left behind.

That record is real in the only sense that matters for a study book: every stand-up status, poker round, issue,
merge request, pipeline log, quad and survey answer quoted in these chapters was produced by running the kit's own
tools on the Tidewater scenario, and the numbers are cited from those files rather than typed. The files are in
`docs/tidewater/` beside the book; its `README.md` lists every one with a line on what it shows. When a chapter says
a pipeline failed, you can open the log. When it says the team committed so many points, the number on the page was
read from the sprint's scorecard when the book was built.

Each sprint chapter has the same shape, so you can read them in order or jump to the one that matches where your own
team is:

- **The question and the mood.** Every sprint answered one systems engineering question, and the pulse survey at the
  retrospective measured how the team felt about the answer.
- **The kickoff.** The goal, the capacity, the plan, the planning-poker rounds, and the learning budget.
- **Week one and week two, day by day.** The stand-ups as they were posted, the GitLab work as it happened, and the
  thought process behind each decision: what the team chose, what else it could have done, and what it would do
  differently now.
- **Refinement, review and retrospective.** What was estimated for the next sprint, what was shown to management and
  how it landed, and what the team changed.
- **The numbers.** The scorecard at the end of the sprint, and what Jim told management.
- **You can now..., exercises, and applying this to your project.**

Two warnings before you start. First, the team is not good at this yet. In sprint 1 their stand-up statuses fail the
lint, their first merge request has no pipeline, and their first local pipeline is red. In sprint 2 their first real
pipeline dies on its first line and their demo loses the room. That is the point: the book shows what a first attempt
actually looks like, so yours does not have to be perfect to be on track. Second, the early sprints are mostly about
the shop rather than the model. The bottom line up front ([[bluf]]) shows how quickly that changes; by sprint 4 most
of the board is systems engineering.
