The Solutions Architect's multi-team scrum operating model

Two-week sprints, several teams, one fixed daily townhall, one architect who owns the master backlog, cross-team alignment, and upward reporting. Every team joins a single Teams call at a fixed time (08:30–08:45) and types their three questions — Y/T/B — into the chat; the chat is the record. Anyone who needs to sync with another team says so on the line and either sets up a follow-up meeting or raises it as a decision to be made — it doesn't get worked live. The architect reads that chat, runs it through the kit in this repo (git bash, vim, Perl), and turns it into journal entries, metrics, and reports for management — no browser dashboard, no separate PM tool, nothing beyond what ships with Git for Windows. Below: the hierarchy of work, the cadences, what is collected at each event, what is produced from it, the metrics and their thresholds, and the feedback loops that make the numbers change behaviour.

1. What you own vs what the teams own

You (architect / chief scrum)Each team (its scrum master / lead)
Tomes and epics, priority orderTask breakdown inside an epic
Master backlog, assignment of work to teamsTeam backlog order, who takes what
Capacity and load across teamsDay-to-day task tracking
Cross-team dependencies and blockersTheir own impediments
Definitions of Ready and DoneApplying them
Metrics, status up and acrossTheir retro actions

The journal records only the architect-level facts: task identity, size, epic, team, sprint, owner, state, blocked. Sub-tasks and hours stay in the teams' own tools or heads. Keep it that way; the value of the journal is that it is small enough to update during a stand-up.

2. Hierarchy of work

Tome    < 2 years, 2–5 live at a time                       tome:  metadata on tasks
Epic    < 13 sprints (26 weeks), one tome, 1+ teams         epic:  metadata on tasks
Task    < 1 sprint (2 weeks), one team, sized in SP         the ledger id (AUTH-104)
Sprint  2 weeks, per team (the timebox, not a size)         Sprint:<N>:<Team>:*

Anything finer-grained than a task (hour-level work inside a day) stays in the team's own tools or heads — it never enters the journal. The task is the smallest unit tracked here, judged strictly pass/fail against its Definition of Done: no partial credit, split it instead.

Points flow through accounts; the account a task's points sit in is its state:

Backlog:Master  →  Backlog:<Team>  →  Sprint:N:<Team>:Committed  →  Done
                                                                →  Carryover  →  next sprint's Committed
                                                                →  Removed

Rules that keep this honest:

  • A task enters once, sized, with id, epic, prio, and ideally tome. (new AUTH-104 5 MFA enrolment p:1 e:Auth in a stand-up file, or an intake block.)
  • Re-sizing posts the difference (est ID N); history is never rewritten.
  • Nothing leaves a sprint except to Done, Carryover, or Removed. Carryover is visible forever.
  • An epic is "done" when its tasks are, so epic progress is derived, never typed.

3. Tempo: the two-week cycle

Day numbering assumes sprints start Wednesday (planning) and end Tuesday (review) so the review-to-planning turn happens inside one day and Mondays/Fridays absorb holidays.

Day  1 Wed   Planning (per team, 30–45 min each), capacity set, commitments made
Day  2 Thu   Townhall begins; dependency check across teams
Day  3 Fri   Townhall; weekly status mail (short)
Day  6 Mon   Townhall; mid-sprint refinement of next sprint's candidates (per team, 30 min)
Day  7 Tue   Townhall
Day  8 Wed   Townhall; mid-sprint health check: load, blocked age, burn vs plan
Day  9 Thu   Townhall; backlog refinement for Master (you + leads, 45 min): size, split, assign
Day 10 Fri   Townhall; weekly status mail
Day 13 Mon   Townhall; pre-review: confirm what will demo, sweep obvious carryover early
Day 14 Tue   Review/demo (all teams, 60 min), retro (per team, 30 min), sprint closed in journal;
             sprint report mailed; velocity recomputed; next capacity drafted

Longer cadences layered on top:

Weekly     Fri  status mail to leadership (metrics + risks), attendance CSV rolled into Excel
Sprint     Tue  review pack; velocity/predictability trend; epic burn
Monthly    first review of the month: epic and tome roll-up, intake vs done, backlog age
Quarterly  tome reset: what is done, what is dropped, what enters; recalibrate capacity

4. The daily townhall (all teams, one Teams call, 0830–0845, mostly typing)

This is the primary battle rhythm. One fixed-time recurring Teams meeting covers every team, every day, 08:30–08:45 sharp — not a round of separate per-team stand-ups. Talking is the exception; the chat is the record. Cameras optional, mics muted unless called on. If two people need to sort something out, they say so on the line and either take it to a follow-up meeting or it becomes a `note`/blocker for a decision — it does not get worked live in the townhall.

08:15  daily.pl cards            each member's card (their sprint items, blockers, yesterday's
                                 open blocker, the Y/T/B reminder) goes out: `cards --send` by
                                 Outlook mail, or the cards page with one "send in Teams" link
                                 per person (opens a 1:1 chat pre-filled; you press Enter)
08:30  daily.pl attend           → roster and declines from the calendar into today's file
08:30  join; you post one line:  "Y/T/B in chat. Say so on the line if you need to sync with
                                 someone — set up a meeting or raise it as a decision. Leave
                                 unless tagged."
08:30–08:38  members type Y/T/B  in the shared chat, across all teams; members who are done
                                 and untagged leave; cross-team asks get flagged, not solved here
08:38  you read the chat; @mention anyone whose answer needs a word; 60 seconds each
08:40  #est / #vote rounds       for tasks refined the day before; numbers in chat, no
                                 discussion unless no consensus, then two minutes and one re-vote
08:43  leads only: dependencies, blockers older than two days, capacity changes
08:45  end. Attendance tab -> download -> standups/<date>-attendance.csv

08:50  copy chat -> :SChat -> :SAnswers, :STally, :SJoined, :SCompile, :SReport, :SAi, :SDraft, commit

What Perl gets from the townhall: every member's Y/T/B (split to teams by owner), estimates and votes, who answered (attendance.csv: answered), who joined and left and for how long (attendance.csv: present/brief with join/leave/minutes), and the suggested journal lines. Your job at the keyboard is reading the chat, running the pipeline, and doing the analysis and reporting — not collecting status by talking.

Rules that make it work:

  • The cards are the agenda. A member who has read their card needs no introduction.
  • Y/T/B first, always, even if you have nothing to say: "B: none" is data.
  • Leaving early is expected. Staying is for coordination that needs a voice.
  • A cross-team integration need is said on the line, then becomes a follow-up meeting or a logged decision item — never worked live in the fifteen minutes.
  • Anything longer than two minutes of talk becomes a note and a follow-up meeting.
  • Estimation happens on refined tasks only; unrefined ones get #est deferred to Day 9.

Planning (Day 1) uses the same townhall slot extended to 45 minutes: cards show each team's ready list, leads post cap N, #est for anything unsized, then commit lines typed by you as leads confirm in chat. Review (Day 14) is the opposite: 60–90 minutes, cameras on, demos with slides, no chat discipline; the journal entry for it is one done/carry/drop sweep afterwards.

4a. Variant: separate per-team stand-ups instead of one townhall

If a program can't get every team onto one 15-minute call (time zones, size, culture), the same pipeline runs per team instead — it is slower and loses the live cross-team "say so on the line" moment, so prefer the joint townhall above whenever it's an option.

08:45  daily.pl attend           → roster and declines from the calendar into today's file
09:00  Alpha stand-up 15 min     → participants type Y/T/B in the Teams chat; you type only
                                   exceptions live in :SNew's file (new / est / drop / cap)
09:15  copy chat → :SChatTeam Alpha, paste
09:20  Bravo stand-up            → same
...    (all teams by 10:00)
10:00  :SAnswers                 → Y/T/B per person recorded; flags; done/block/absent lines suggested
       :SChat + :STally          → if estimates/votes were taken in chat
       confirm the suggested lines (uncomment), :SCompile → journal updated, errors caught now
       :SAi                      → prompt to your AI service (paste, or curl); reply → :SAiResponse
       :SReport                  → dashboard + status text/html (marked)
       daily.pl post             → metrics into today's stand-up meeting bodies
       :SDraft                   → status draft to leads, review, send
       git commit                → daily.pl all --draft does compile+report+draft+commit
10:15  done.

Inputs are the same either way: only what changed — done, carry, block/unblock, assign, est, new, note/risk/absent. Names and sizes come from the journal; nobody re-types anything.

8. Feedback loops (what changes because of a number)

  1. Blocked → escalation. Every blocker is in the daily mail with its age until unblock. Three days is your cue to act across teams; five days means it is on the weekly mail by name.
  2. Velocity → commitment cap. At planning, a team's commitment is capped at its 3-sprint average velocity unless the lead argues a specific reason. Load % is printed so the argument is about a number.
  3. Carryover → task size. Carryover rate above 20% sends the team's tasks back to refinement to be split before they are estimated again.
  4. Estimate spread → refinement. A #est round without consensus and with outliers means the task is discussed, not averaged; the tally names who to ask. Re-estimate next stand-up.
  5. Epic burn → re-plan. Remaining epic points ÷ velocity gives sprints-to-done; when that passes the epic's target, you re-scope (drop), re-assign across teams, or move the date, and say which in the sprint report.
  6. Intake vs done → conversation upward. When intake exceeds done for three sprints, the backlog-depth number goes into the weekly mail with the sentence "at current velocity this is N sprints of work"; it is leadership's decision what to cut.
  7. Attendance → meeting hygiene. Declines and absences accumulate in attendance.csv; a pattern by person or day means the stand-up time is wrong for that team, not that people are lazy.
  8. Retro actions → notes next sprint. Retro outputs are note retro: lines in the review day's file; they show up in that day's mail and you re-read them at the next retro.

9. Definitions

Ready (a task may be committed): has an id, size ≤ 13, epic, acceptance criteria (in the team's tool), no unresolved dependency on another team's uncommitted task, and an owner at planning.

Done (points move to Done): acceptance criteria met, merged/integrated, demonstrable at review, no known blocker. Partial credit does not exist; split the task instead.

Removed: scope dropped with a note saying why. Removed points never count as done and are excluded from epic totals.

10. Backlog management

Master backlog (Backlog:Master) is yours. Ordered by prio within tome; sized at least roughly (T-shirt → SP at refinement). Day 9 refinement moves the top tasks to team backlogs (refine ID under the team's section; prune ID reason retires the stale ones) (Backlog:<Team>) with an owner where known. Keep 2–4 sprints of sized work per team ahead; prune anything older than a quarter that nobody argues for.

Team backlogs are ordered by the team; you only watch depth and age. A team backlog below one sprint of work means you failed to refine; above four means you assigned too far ahead and the priorities will change under it.

Sprint backlog is the Committed account; it changes during a sprint only through new! (interrupt work, always tagged so you can measure it), drop, and carry. Interrupt SP per sprint is a metric worth adding to the weekly mail if it is not near zero.

Tomes and epics: every task carries epic:; epics carry a tome: on at least one task (the roll-up picks the first it sees). epics shows done / in-sprint / backlog per epic; the quarterly review is that table plus a decision per row: continue, cut, or add.

12. Anti-patterns this model is designed to prevent

  • Metrics typed into slides by hand. Everything here is derived from the journal, so it cannot disagree with itself and takes zero minutes to regenerate.
  • Re-typing task titles, sizes, or names anywhere. Once, at intake.
  • Carryover hidden by re-creating tasks. Points are conserved; bal Sprint:41 shows the truth.
  • Status mails that are prose. The mail is the table plus blockers plus three notes.
  • Stand-ups that become status reports to you. Your inputs are verbs; the conversation is theirs.
