3.  Before: finding where the prime's DOORS text and the model
disagreed was never done systematically, and a model review was a
sixty-slide briefing of screenshots that took three weeks. Now:
the pipeline compares every requirement's text on every change in
under a minute, and the model site is current every night.

4.  What did not go well: in sprint 3 a hand edit broke the
grammar check and the runner hardening waited on the ISSM with no
date set, and the pulse survey dipped from 2.4 to 2.2. The one
ungated change was the first one, merged before the pipeline
existed. Verification coverage stayed at 21 of 30, because the
gaps are the prime's to fix; they are reported as discrepancy
reports. What the team did: it fixed the edit on its branch
before it reached the model, asked the ISSM for dated review
slots, and kept reporting the low numbers.

5.  For the next milestone, the January drop from the prime, this
means the team can show what the drop changes for the government
model within a day of receiving it. The remaining risk is
behavior modeling, which the converter does not carry and the
team writes by hand.

6.  Request approval of the second runner machine and of the
GitLab tier decision by 15 January 2027, as asked in the
memorandum of 21 October 2026.
