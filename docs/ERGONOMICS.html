Ergonomics

Keystrokes and mouse movements per person per day, measured on the loop as built, then redesigned for a keyboard-only architect and for team members who start needing help and end needing none.

Method

One keystroke = one key or chord (Ctrl-V counts 1). A mouse movement = moving the pointer to a target and clicking; window switches by mouse count as one movement each. Typed text is counted by characters. Counts are for one townhall with 10 teams, roughly 100 statuses, 8 corrections and 3 blockers to work — the volume the kit is built for.

The one mouse movement left is clicking into the Teams compose box, which Teams needs. A thumbs-up reaction is replaced by typing +1 (or y, ok, 👍 as text) — the parsers treat an ack from someone with a proposal as accepting it, so the status is recorded as confirmed, not assumed.

The principle behind the numbers

  • Never make the architect cross a window by mouse. Every window the loop needs is a Vim window; Teams is one Alt-Tab away and only ever gets Ctrl-A Ctrl-C or Ctrl-V Enter.
  • The team's cost falls as the kit learns them. Proposals turn a status into an acknowledgement; read-backs fade after five clean days; the tone of a reply is set by what the person did, not by a global switch.
  • Help is per person and per day, and it is on the record. Who needed a correction, who was silent, who accepted a proposal: all of it is in the answers files, so coaching is a fact, not a memory.
